# Lighthouses and the Fresnel lens

Before satellite navigation, lighthouses were the most reliable way to find a safe passage along a dangerous coast at night. Each lighthouse has its own light characteristic, a pattern of flashes that tells sailors exactly which light they are looking at.

The Fresnel lens, designed by Augustin-Jean Fresnel in 1822, replaced heavy solid lenses with concentric rings of glass prisms. It captured far more light from a small flame and projected a beam visible for more than twenty nautical miles.

The word pharos, from the island near Alexandria that held one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, still names lighthouses in many languages: faro in Spanish and Italian, phare in French and farol in Portuguese.

Today most lighthouses are automated and use LED lamps, but many are preserved as museums and historical landmarks.
