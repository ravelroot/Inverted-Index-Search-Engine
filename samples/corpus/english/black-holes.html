Black holes

A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape. Its boundary is called the event horizon.

Stellar black holes form when massive stars collapse at the end of their lives. Supermassive black holes, millions to billions of times the mass of the Sun, sit at the centers of most galaxies, including the Milky Way.

Astronomers detect black holes through their effects: stars orbiting an invisible companion, glowing accretion disks that emit X-rays, and gravitational waves from merging pairs. In 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope released the first image of a black hole shadow, in the galaxy M87.
